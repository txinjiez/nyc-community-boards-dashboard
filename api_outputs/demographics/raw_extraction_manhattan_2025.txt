Manhattan Community Boards Demographic Report for 2025
Pursuant to New York City Charter Section 82 (17)(a), the Office of the Manhattan Borough
President (MBPO) submits this report on the application and selection processes for the
appointment of community board members for the 2025-2027 term.
Introduction and Vision for Manhattan Community Boards
Community boards are an essential form of local democracy, providing local community
members and stakeholders with a volunteer opportunity to help govern their neighborhood and
city. Composed of 50 volunteer members serving staggered two-year terms, community boards
are tasked with reviewing and making recommendations on complex, local issues impacting
education, parks and open space, libraries, housing, transportation, economic development,
zoning, the environment and city planning, among other critical aspects of urban living. In
addition to appointing over 300 members (including vacant mid-year vacancies) to the
Borough’s 12 community boards each year, the MBPO also works to provide professional
support and extensive trainings to board members and staff in order to increase efficiency and
impact.
In keeping with Borough President Levine’s commitment to ensure community boards reflect the
diversity of our neighborhoods, the MBPO has continued to seek out Manhattanites who have
historically been less represented on community boards, including but not limited to youth 16
years or older, public housing residents, and people holding marginalized identities. This
commitment guided every aspect of the appointment process, from outreach, to review and final
determinations.
The MBPO is enormously proud of our 2025 appointment cycle. Over 950 applicants and 570
interviews to fill 320 seats were conducted, and this year’s appointments reflect significant
progress toward boards that better represent the diversity of their communities. Over 70% of new
members self-identified as a race other than white/non-Hispanic alone, and 23% self-identified
as Latino/Hispanic. The new class also includes strong LGBTQIA+ representation, gender
diversity, and a notable number of parents. Geographic diversity across the borough was also
made a priority in the selection process.
Community Board Application and Outreach Strategy
The 2025 Manhattan Community Board application gathered a wide range of information used to
ensure appointments reflected the needs and interests of the communities they served. In addition
to standard demographic and residency questions, the application included questions on
applicants’ civic engagement, community priorities, education, skills, car ownership, housing
type and more. Additionally, to better assess each individual’s approach to key community
issues, new applicants and reapplicants were asked to rank the importance of elements they
consider important in the creation of housing, like affordability, infrastructure impact, and open
space, as well as social services and outreach. Also included are questions to ensure each

applicant is committed to attending all meetings to the best of one’s ability. It was also made
fully available in Spanish and Traditional Chinese.
On January 13, 2025, the application was published on the MBPO website
(www.manhattanbp.nyc.gov), with a deadline of February 28, 2025 at 5pm (this deadline was
later extended to March 7, 2025 to allow time for additional applicants to apply). Direct
correspondence was made between the MBPO, District Managers, and community board
Chairpersons to alert them of the application and request their assistance in sharing the
application link among their members and larger community lists. Members whose terms were
concluding were also emailed and called directly by MBPO staff. Borough President Levine and
staff also promoted the application at Borough Board and Borough Service Cabinet meetings in
January, February and March, as well as at monthly district service cabinet and full board
meetings for all 12 Manhattan community boards.
Additionally, the MBPO utilized a wide range of tools to further promote the application to
individuals with diverse backgrounds. The Community Affairs Unit of MBPO reached out to
public housing tenant leaders, community-based organizations, public high schools, not-for-
profit organizations, organized labor and other community-based groups in order to both
promote the application as well as present it to their communities. The MBPO also requested the
assistance of all Manhattan City Council Members to further promote the application throughout
their districts and e-mail listservs. The MBPO created a series of digital ads that ran on
LinkNYC towers throughout the Borough, and the applications were covered in local news
media online and in print. Borough President Levine also attended various community meetings
and participated in interviews with local news outlets encouraging Manhattan residents to apply.
For the term beginning in 2025, the MBPO received a total of 951 applicants, inclusive of both
new applicants and reapplicants. This is up significantly from the 837 total applicants we
received in 2024.As in previous years, the MBPO created a series of LinkNYC ads that ran
throughout the borough. The ads ran until the end of the application period on March 7th (see
image below of a sample of LinkNYC advertisements that ran in the 2025 application period). In
addition, the MBPO ran additional digital advertising on its social media platforms (Twitter,
Instagram and Facebook), using the same series of advertisements appearing on LinkNYC
kiosks.

Community Board Application Review and Appointment Process
There were 122 vacancies in total on the 12 community boards during the 2025 appointment
cycle. A table of this disaggregated information can be found below. It is important to note that
the overall number of vacancies has continued to fluctuate, as board membership can change
dynamically based on removals or resignations throughout the year. It is also important to note
that when considering community board appointments, the Borough President considered not
only vacancies, but took steps to consider the applicants for all 300 seats that were either open or
up for reappointment.

MBPO staff compared each district’s census data profile (as provided by the Department of City
Planning) to the demographic makeup of each community board. This, along with direct input
from District Managers, helped determine whether the aggregate of appointments fairly represent
all segments of the community, and where particular attention should be put during the
appointment process.
In the 2025 application cycle, every new and returning board member application was read and
carefully considered by MBPO staff and the essay portions of every application were reviewed
by at least two MBPO staff members.
After the application portals closed, a digitized version of all applications for each council
district was distributed to every Manhattan City Council Member. Council Members received
copies of all applications that resided, worked or had a significant interest in their districts. The
digitized applications included the full application as well as any additional documents (such as a
resume or curriculum vitae) that were submitted. Borough President Levine and MBPO staff also
met individually with Council members and their staff to review applicants and community
board vacancies.

In addition to submitting application materials, the MBPO requires that all applicants, both new
and re-applying, participate in the interview portion of the review process. The interview process
consisted of a 2-hour virtual exercise in which applicants were randomly placed in break-out
rooms with approximately 10-15 other applicants. While in these breakout rooms, the applicants
were asked to consider and discuss two community board scenarios - one focusing on preference
of funds allocation in the City budget and the other focusing on a land use proposal. At least two
members of MBPO staff were assigned to each room and were asked to review each applicant
and complete a digital evaluation form at the end of each interview session. Applicants were
reviewed on their ability to communicate, active listening skills, ability to identify problems and
come up with creative solutions, to respectfully engage with others, collaborate, and establish
next steps. In the 2025 interview cycle, MBPO offered seven interview dates between March 13
to March 19, 2025, with at least one session on every day of the week and at various times in
order to accommodate as many schedules as possible. Each session was capped at 120
participants. In total, 568 applicants participated in the interview process. Disaggregated tables
of applications received as well as interviews conducted for 2024 and 2025 are below.

Demographics
The MBPO maintains anonymized demographic information disaggregated by community board
on the office’s website (manhattanbp.nyc.gov). The following information below describes the
demographic information that board members voluntarily disclosed as of 2025.
The 2025 appointments also showed significant steps towards being more representative of our
diverse communities. For example, 23% of new applicants identified as Latino/Hispanic, a
demographic that was previously slightly underrepresented. In fact, just over 70% of new
members identified as a race other than white/non-Hispanic alone.
Additionally, the class of new appointments included over 13% of individuals that identified as
LGBTQIA+; 50% identified as male, 48% female, 1% gender non-conforming, and 1% prefer
not to say; 22% of new appointments identified as parents. We also prioritized geographic
representation and diversity across the borough.
