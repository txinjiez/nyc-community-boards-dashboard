Pursuant to New York City Charter section 82(17)(a) the Office of the Manhattan Borough
President submits this report on the application and selection processes for the appointment of
community board members for the 2022-24 term and the 2023-2025 term.
Introduction and Vision for Manhattan Community Boards
Community boards are an essential form of local democracy, providing local community
members and stakeholders with opportunities to govern their neighborhood and city . Composed
of 50 volunteer members serving staggered two-year terms, community boards are tasked with
reviewing and making recommendations on complex, local issues impacting education, parks
and open space, libraries, housing, transportation, economic development, zoning, the
environment and city planning, among other critical aspects of urban living. In addition to
appointing 300 members to the Borough’s 12 community boards each year, the Manhattan
Borough President’s Office (MBPO) also works to provide professional support and training to
the boards in order to increase efficiency and impact.
The MBPO firmly believes in the pivotal role community boards play in shaping the future of
their districts. In keeping with Borough President Levine’s commitment to better supporting
communities that have been historically overlooked and ensure community boards adequately
reflect the diversity of our neighborhoods, the MBPO has continued to seek out Manhattanites
who have historically been less represented on community boards, including but not limited to
youth 16 years or older, public housing residents, and people holding marginalized identities.
Borough President Levine is committed to ensuring that community boards authentically reflect
the diversity of the neighborhoods they represent, lifting up the lived experience and concerns of
Manhattanites who have long been under-represented.
The goals of diversity and equity guided the community board application outreach, the review
process, and final appointments.
Community Board Application and Outreach Strategy
Borough President Mark Levine took office on January 1, 2022, and promptly began the process
of creating and publishing the 2022 Manhattan Community Board application. In addition to
asking applicants questions on demographic information including name and address, residence,
business professional, other significant interest in the community district, age, gender, race,
ethnicity, sexual orientation, disability status, languages spoken and other demographic
information that applicants chose to disclose, the 2022 application also attempted to capture
additional information on educational attainment, special skills, car ownership, and community
involvement. Both new and re-applicants were asked quantitative and qualitative questions about
their civic engagement, needs of their communities, and goals for their time of potential service
on the board.
On January 12, 2022, the application was published on the MBPO website
(www.manhattanbp.nyc.gov), with a deadline of March 1, 2022. Direct correspondence was
made between the MBPO, District Managers, and community board Chairpersons to alert them

vacancies on all community boards. Upon consultation with community board District Managers
and Chairs, it was confirmed that there were 87 vacancies in total on the 12 community boards at
the beginning of the 2022 appointment cycle. A table of this disaggregated information can be
found below. In the 2023 appointment cycle, there were 107 vacancies. A table of this
disaggregated information can be found below. It is important to note that the overall number of
vacancies has continued to fluctuate, as board membership is dynamic based on removals or
resignations throughout the year. It is also important to note that when considering community
board appointments, the Borough President looked not only at vacancies, but took steps to
consider the applicants for all 300 seats that were either open or up for reappointment.

MBPO staff utilized each district’s census data profile as provided by the Department of City
Planning compared to the demographic makeup of each community board to help determine
whether the aggregate of appointments fairly represent all segments of the community. Staff also
received input from community board District Managers with regard to geographic
representation, as well as other diversity needs, including more participation from public housing
residents, working parents, people living with disabilities, small business owners, veterans, and
people holding marginalized identities.
In both the 2022 and 2023 application cycle, every new and returning board member application
was read and carefully considered by MBPO staff. In most cases, the essay portions of every
application were reviewed by at least two MBPO staff.
After the application portals closed, a digitized version of all applications for each council district
was distributed to every Manhattan City Council Member. Council Members received copies of
all applications that resided in their districts. The digitized applications included the full
application as well as any additional documents (such as a resume or curriculum vitae) that were
submitted. In both the 2022 and 2023 cycle, Borough President Levine and MBPO staff also met
individually with Councilmembers to review applicants and community board vacancies.
Wanting to give every applicant full consideration, the MBPO staff offered all applicants the
opportunity to participate in the interview portion of the review process. The interview process
consisted of a 2-hour virtual exercises in which applicants were randomly placed in break-out
rooms with approximately 10-15 other applicants. While in these breakout rooms, the applicants
were asked to consider and discuss two community board scenarios - one focusing on land use
and the other focusing on City budget. At least two volunteer evaluators (with at least one being
from MBPO staff and the other being a volunteer staff member from a Manhattan City Council
Member office) were assigned to each room and were asked to review each applicant and
complete a digital evaluation form at the end of each interview session. Applicants were

For both the 2022 and 2023 application cycle, after the interview sessions were complete, MBPO
staff used information from the submitted application as well as the reviews from every
applicant’s interview session to create a list of recommended appointees for each community
board. In nearly every board, the list of recommended candidates surpassed the number of
vacancies. The Borough President reviewed staff recommendations, as well as the submitted
applications before making final selections for board appointment.
Demographics
The MBPO has maintained anonymized demographic information disaggregated by community
board on the office’s website (manhattanbp.nyc.gov). The following information below describes
the demographic information that board members voluntarily disclosed as of December 2022, as
well as May 2023.
While the MBPO is very proud of the 2022 and 2023 classes of appointed board members with
regard to the diversity of voices and experiences, there is still much work to be done to increase
broad representation on Manhattan community boards. We strongly believe that with future
appointment processes, expanded outreach, and thoughtful consideration of all applicants,
Manhattan community boards can become a more powerful tool in uplifting overlooked and
underserved communities.
