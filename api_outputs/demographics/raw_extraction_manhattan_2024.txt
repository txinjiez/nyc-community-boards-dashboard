Hon. Mark Levine, Manhattan Borough President
Manhattan Community Boards Demographic Report for 2024
Pursuant to New York City Charter section 82 (17)(a), the Office of the Manhattan Borough
President submits this report on the application and selection processes for the appointment of
community board members for the 2024-2026 term.
Introduction and Vision for Manhattan Community Boards
Community boards are an essential form of local democracy, providing local community
members and stakeholders with a volunteer opportunity to govern their neighborhood and city.
Composed of 50 volunteer members serving staggered two-year terms, community boards are
tasked with reviewing and making recommendations on complex, local issues impacting
education, parks and open space, libraries, housing, transportation, economic development,
zoning, the environment and city planning, among other critical aspects of urban living. In
addition to appointing 300 members to the Borough’s 12 community boards each year, the
Manhattan Borough President’s Office (MBPO) also works to provide professional support and
extensive trainings to board members and staff in order to increase efficiency and impact.
The MBPO firmly believes in the pivotal role community boards play in shaping the future of
their districts. In keeping with Borough President Levine’s commitment to better supporting
communities that have been historically overlooked and ensure community boards adequately
reflect the diversity of our neighborhoods, the MBPO has continued to seek out Manhattanites
who have historically been less represented on community boards, including but not limited to
youth 16 years or older, public housing residents, and people holding marginalized identities.
Borough President Levine is committed to ensuring that community boards authentically reflect
the diversity of the neighborhoods they represent, lifting up the lived experience and concerns of
Manhattanites who have long been underrepresented.
The goals of diversity and equity guided the community board application outreach, review
process, and final appointments.
Community Board Application and Outreach Strategy
Borough President Mark Levine took office on January 1, 2022, and promptly began the process
of creating and publishing the 2022 Manhattan Community Board application. In addition to
asking applicants questions on demographic information including name and address, residence,
business professional, other significant interest in the community district, age, gender, race,
ethnicity, sexual orientation, disability status, languages spoken and other demographic
information that applicants chose to disclose, our inaugural 2022 application also attempted to
capture additional information on educational attainment, special skills, car ownership, and
community involvement. Both new and re-applicants were asked quantitative and qualitative
questions about their civic engagement, needs of their communities, and goals for their time of
potential service on the board. We have since updated the application to better acquire
individuals looking to address current concerns in the borough as well as ensure the board is able
to achieve its objectives. The 2024 application includes a new quantitative and qualitative

On January 11, 2024, the application was published on the MBPO website

January, February and March, as well as at monthly district service cabinet and full board
meetings for all 12 Manhattan community boards.
Additionally, MBPO utilized a wide range of tools to further promote the application. The
Community Affairs Unit of MBPO reached out to public housing tenant leaders, community-
based organizations, public high schools, not-for-profit organizations and other community-
based groups in order to both promote the application as well as present to their tenants. The
MBPO also requested the assistance of all Manhattan City Council Members to further promote
the application throughout their districts and listservs. The MBPO created a digital ad that ran on
LinkNYC towers throughout the Borough, and purchased advertisements in local newspapers
and social media. Borough President Levine also attended various community meetings and
participated in interviews with local news outlets encouraging Manhattan residents to apply.
The MBPO decided to extend the application for two additional weeks in order to increase the
overall number of applicants, with a new deadline of March 8, 2024. This extension was
announced using all of the aforementioned community outlets. For the term beginning in 2024,
the MBPO received a total of 838 entries, inclusive of both new applicants and reapplicants.
In 2024, the MBPO created a series of LinkNYC ads that ran throughout the borough. The ads
ran until the end of the application period on March 8th (see image below of a sample of
LinkNYC advertisements that ran in the 2024 application period). In addition, the MBPO
purchased additional digital advertising on social media platforms (Twitter, Instagram and
Facebook), using the same series of advertisements running on LinkNYC kiosks.

Community Board Application Review and Appointment Process
While the application portal was still open, the MBPO staff began the process of assessing
vacancies on all community boards. Upon consultation with community board District Managers
and Chairs, it was confirmed that there were 87 vacancies in total on the 12 community boards at
the beginning of the 2022 appointment cycle. A table of this disaggregated information can be
found below. In the 2023 appointment cycle, there were 107 vacancies. A table of this
disaggregated information can be found below. It is important to note that the overall number of
vacancies has continued to fluctuate, as board membership is dynamic based on removals or
resignations throughout the year. It is also important to note that when considering community
board appointments, the Borough President looked not only at vacancies, but took steps to
consider the applicants for all 300 seats that were either open or up for reappointment.

MBPO staff utilized each district’s census data profile as provided by the Department of City
Planning compared to the demographic makeup of each community board to help determine
whether the aggregate of appointments fairly represent all segments of the community. Staff also
received input from community board District Managers with regard to geographic
representation, as well as other diversity needs, including more participation from public housing
residents, working parents, people living with disabilities, small business owners, veterans, and
people holding marginalized identities.
In the 2024 application cycle, every new and returning board member application was read and
carefully considered by MBPO staff. In most cases, the essay portions of every application were
reviewed by at least two MBPO staff members.
After the application portals closed, a digitized version of all applications for each council
district was distributed to every Manhattan City Council Member. Council Members received
copies of all applications that resided, worked or had a significant interest in their districts. The
digitized applications included the full application as well as any additional documents (such as a
resume or curriculum vitae) that were submitted. Borough President Levine and MBPO staff also
met individually with Councilmembers to review applicants and community board vacancies.
Wanting to give every applicant full consideration, the MBPO staff offered all applicants, both
new and re-applying, the opportunity to participate in the interview portion of the review
process. The interview process consisted of a 2-hour virtual exercise in which applicants were
randomly placed in break-out rooms with approximately 10-15 other applicants. While in these
breakout rooms, the applicants were asked to consider and discuss two community board
scenarios - one focusing on land use and the other focusing on City budget. At least two
volunteer evaluators (with at least one being from MBPO staff and the other being a volunteer
staff member from a Manhattan City Council Member office) were assigned to each room and
were asked to review each applicant and complete a digital evaluation form at the end of each
interview session. Applicants were reviewed on their ability to communicate, active listening,
ability to identify problems and come up with creative solutions, to respectfully engage with
others, collaborate, and establish next steps. In the 2024 interview cycle, MBPO offered 7
interview dates between March 13 to March 19, 2024, with at least one session on every day of
the week and at various times in order to accommodate as many schedules as possible. Each
session was capped at 110 participants. In total, 609 applicants participated in the interview
process. Disaggregated tables of applications received as well as interviews conducted for 2023
and 2024 are below.

For the 2024 application cycle, after the interview sessions were complete, MBPO staff used
information from the submitted application as well as the reviews from every applicant’s
interview session to create a list of recommended appointees for each community board. In
nearly every board, the list of recommended candidates surpassed the number of vacancies. The
Borough President reviewed staff recommendations, as well as the submitted applications before
making final selections for board appointment.
Demographics
The MBPO has maintained anonymized demographic information disaggregated by community
board on the office’s website (manhattanbp.nyc.gov). The following information below describes
the demographic information that board members voluntarily disclosed as of 2024.
While the MBPO is very proud of the 2022, 2023 and 2024 classes of appointed board members
with regard to the diversity of voices and experiences, there is still much work to be done to
increase broad representation on Manhattan community boards. We strongly believe that with
future appointment processes, expanded outreach, additional advertisement in languages other
than English, and thoughtful consideration of all applicants, Manhattan community boards can
become a more powerful tool in uplifting often overlooked and underserved communities.
